# Grimwild vs. Archon: Action Resolution Comparison

## Grimwild Action Resolution Flow

### Basic Flow (3-5 Steps)

1. **Frame the action** (player describes goal + approach)
2. **Determine pool** (roll stat dice [1-3d6] + thorns [d8s for difficulty])
3. **Roll & read highest d6:**
   - 6 = Perfect (success, no trouble)
   - 4-5 = Messy (success + GM makes impact move)
   - 1-3 = Grim (failure + GM makes impact move)
   - Multiple 6s = Critical (Perfect + choose bonus: Greater Effect / Secondary Effect / Setup)
4. **Apply thorns:** Each 7-8 on d8s "cuts" result down one tier (Perfect → Messy → Grim → Disaster)
5. **Resolve impact move** (if prompted by Messy/Grim/Disaster)

**That's it. 3-5 steps total.**

---

## Archon Action Resolution Flow

### Full Flow (10 Steps)

1. **Frame action** (GM sets Stakes, Vantage)
2. **Determine pool** (Approach + Trait if invoked)
3. **Apply Boons/Banes** (circumstantial, capped at ±2)
4. **Push Yourself?** (mark 1 Stress for +1 Boon)
5. **Roll dice** (2d6 per die in pool)
6. **Determine raw outcome** (Triumph/Success/Conflict/Setback)
7. **Spend Edge** (if available, cancel Complications before they apply)
8. **Apply remaining Complications** (each reduces outcome by 1 tier)
9. **Apply Expertise** (if final outcome is Setback, convert to Conflict)
10. **Apply final outcome** (Consequence + mark tracks)

**That's 10 steps, with 5 modifying subsystems.**

---

## Key Differences

| Aspect | Grimwild | Archon |
|--------|----------|---------|
| **Base dice** | 1-3d6 (stat rating) | 2-5d6 (Approach + modifiers) |
| **Difficulty** | Thorns (d8s) added to pool | Boons/Banes modify pool size (±2 cap) |
| **Enemy defenses** | Built into thorns or fiction | Complication tags (Resilient, Deadly, etc.) |
| **Success levels** | 3 (Perfect/Messy/Grim) + Disaster | 4 (Triumph/Success/Conflict/Setback) |
| **Post-roll modifiers** | Just thorns cutting | Edge (cancel Complications) + Expertise (convert Setback) |
| **Consequences** | GM makes "impact move" (freeform) | Structured Consequence tiers (1 Stress, 2 Stress, Afflictions) |
| **Resource spend** | Spark (add dice before roll) | Momentum (Create an Opening = +1 Boon + 1 Edge) |
| **Progress tracking** | Diminishing Pools (roll all, remove 1-3s) | Fixed Track sizes (mark 1 box per success) |

---

## Resolution Step Count

### Grimwild: 3 Steps (Baseline)
1. Frame + Pool
2. Roll + Read Highest
3. Apply Thorns (if any cut)

**Optional additions:**
- +1 step if Messy/Grim (GM makes impact move)
- +1 step if spending Spark (add dice)

**Maximum: 5 steps**

---

### Archon: 10 Steps (Baseline)
1. Frame
2. Determine Pool
3. Apply Boons/Banes
4. Push Yourself? (optional but common)
5. Roll
6. Raw Outcome
7. Edge (if applicable)
8. Complications (if applicable)
9. Expertise (if applicable)
10. Final Outcome + Consequences

**Every action goes through all 10 steps (though some may be "N/A").**

---

## Where Complexity Lives

### Grimwild Complexity

**Pre-roll:**
- Vantage assessment (is this Certain/Reasonable/Doubtful/Impossible?)
- Thorn count (GM judgment: how many d8s?)
- Spark spending (add dice?)

**Post-roll:**
- Check thorns for 7-8 (cuts)
- GM invents Impact Move (if Messy/Grim)

**Complexity is in GM judgment** (how many thorns? what impact move?), not mechanical subsystems.

---

### Archon Complexity

**Pre-roll:**
- Vantage assessment
- Boons/Banes (circumstantial)
- Push Yourself decision
- Create an Opening decision (spend Momentum for Edge?)

**Post-roll:**
- Edge vs. Complications (which tags apply? how many cancel?)
- Expertise check (does it apply? does it convert?)
- Structured Consequences (1 Stress, 2 Stress, Affliction size)

**Complexity is in mechanical subsystems** (Edge, Expertise, Complications, Consequences), not GM judgment.

---

## Progress Tracking Differences

### Grimwild: Diminishing Pools

**How it works:**
- Start with pool of dice (2d/short, 4d/medium, 8d/long)
- When triggered: roll all dice, remove those showing 1-3
- When pool reaches 0d, task is complete

**Math:**
- 2d clears in ~2.7 rolls
- 4d clears in ~3.5 rolls
- 8d clears in ~4.4 rolls

**Interesting property:** Adding more dice has diminishing returns on duration.

**Player interaction:**
- "Greater Effect" critical bonus: remove 1d before rolling (accelerates dramatically on small pools)
- "Potency": also removes 1d before rolling
- If no dice removed: can Push (mark harm) to remove 1d, or Pivot (get secondary effect instead)

---

### Archon: Fixed Tracks

**How it works:**
- GM sets track size (2/4/6/8+ boxes)
- Each success marks 1 box
- When full, task is complete

**Math:**
- 2-box track: 2 successes
- 4-box track: 4 successes
- 8-box track: 8 successes

**Linear scaling.**

**Player interaction:**
- Enhanced Effect (from Trait): mark 2 boxes instead of 1
- Success rate depends on Complications + modifiers

---

## Enemy Differentiation

### Grimwild

**Enemies are described fictionally, then:**
- GM adds thorns based on situation ("heavily armored" = +1 or +2 thorns)
- Impact moves are freeform based on enemy type
- Task pools sized for durability (2d minion, 4d standard, 6d elite, 8d boss)

**No stat blocks.** Everything flows from fiction → GM judgment → thorns/pools.

**Example: Armored Knight**
- GM: "You're attacking a heavily armored knight. That's 2 thorns."
- Player rolls stat + 2d8
- If Messy/Grim, GM makes impact move: "His counter cuts deep—mark bloodied."

---

### Archon

**Enemies have stat blocks with:**
- Complication tags (Resilient, Deadly, Warded, etc.)
- Track sizes (Harm 4, Will 2 = tough body, weak will)
- Challenge Moves (spend Suspense to activate)

**Mechanical tags determine difficulty.**

**Example: Armored Knight**
- Tags: Resilient (applies to physical attacks), Deadly (applies in combat)
- Harm Track: 4 boxes
- Player attacks: 2 Complications apply (Resilient + Deadly)
  - Success → Conflict → Setback (reduced by 2 Complications)
  - Expertise converts Setback → Conflict (if applicable)
  - Final: Conflict (mark 1 progress + 1 Stress consequence)

## Which System Is Simpler?

### By Raw Step Count

**Grimwild wins:** 3-5 steps vs. 10 steps

---

### By Decision Points

**Grimwild:**
- Should I spend Spark? (before roll)
- If no dice removed from pool: Push or Pivot? (after roll)

**2 decision points.**

**Archon:**
- Should I Push Yourself? (before roll)
- Should I Create an Opening? (before roll)
- Which Edge to spend? (after roll, if applicable)

**3 decision points** (but more impactful choices).

---

### By GM Burden

**Grimwild:**
- Assess Vantage → set thorns (judgment call)
- Invent Impact Move on the fly (creative burden)
- No stat blocks to reference
- More improvisational

**Archon:**
- Assess Vantage → set Boons/Banes (within ±2 cap)
- Reference Complication tags (which apply?)
- Apply structured Consequences (1/2 Stress, Affliction)
- Less improvisational, more mechanical

**Trade-off:** Grimwild = faster but requires GM creativity. Archon = slower but more mechanically consistent.

---

## What Makes Grimwild Simpler?

### 1. No Post-Roll Modification Layers

**Grimwild:** Roll → check thorns → done.

**Archon:** Roll → Edge → Complications → Expertise → done.

The post-roll "modification stack" is Archon's biggest complexity source.

---

### 2. Unified Difficulty System

**Grimwild:** Everything is thorns. Enemy armor? Thorns. Wrong tool? Thorns. Outnumbered? Thorns.

**Archon:** Different systems for different things:
- Circumstantial difficulty = Boons/Banes
- Enemy defenses = Complications
- Specialist mastery = Edge/Expertise

More granular, but also more to track.

---

### 3. Freeform Consequences

**Grimwild:** GM makes an "impact move" (Hit Hard, Complicate Things, Counter, etc.). Creative, flexible.

**Archon:** Structured tiers (1 Stress, 2 Stress, Affliction size 1-3). Consistent, but more bookkeeping.

---

### 4. Diminishing Pools vs. Fixed Tracks

**Grimwild pools have variance:** You might clear a 4d pool in 2 rolls (lucky) or 6 rolls (unlucky).

**Archon tracks are deterministic:** A 4-box track always takes exactly 4 successes (unless Enhanced Effect).

**Variance can feel more dynamic, but also less predictable.**

---

## What Makes Archon More Complex?

### The Five Subsystems Affecting Resolution

1. **Boons/Banes** (pre-roll pool modification)
2. **Complications** (enemy defensive tags)
3. **Edge** (tactical bypass of Complications)
4. **Expertise** (safety net converting Setback)
5. **Momentum economy** (resource spend for Edge + Boon)

Each subsystem serves a purpose, but they stack:
- Boons/Banes tune difficulty
- Complications create tactical puzzles
- Edge rewards positioning/mastery
- Expertise ensures specialists don't fail
- Momentum creates resource decisions

**If you removed any one of these, would the system collapse?**

Let's test:

---

## Thought Experiment: What If We Removed Each Subsystem?

### Remove Boons/Banes

**Result:** No GM difficulty dial. Everything is "roll your pool."

**Impact:** HIGH. GM needs a way to adjust difficulty. Grimwild uses thorns for this.

---

### Remove Complications

**Result:** All enemies are the same defensively. No tactical puzzle (Resilient foe vs. Elusive foe).

**Impact:** MEDIUM. You'd lose enemy differentiation. Could compensate with Vantage (GM adds Banes for "heavily armored"), but then you're back to GM judgment like Grimwild.

---

### Remove Edge

**Result:** No way to bypass Complications tactically. Create an Opening would need a new effect.

**Impact:** MEDIUM. You'd lose the tactical "bypass defenses" layer. Characters with Momentum couldn't tactically solve heavily-defended enemies.

---

### Remove Expertise

**Result:** Specialists fail more often (44% Setback rate at 2d6, 23% at 3d6).

**Impact:** LOW-MEDIUM. Compensatable with Trait granting +1 Boon instead. Success rates change but system still functions.

---

### Remove Momentum (and thus Edge)

**Result:** No resource economy. Push Yourself / Assist / Create an Opening gone.

**Impact:** HIGH. Major strategic layer removed. Game becomes more swingy, less player agency.

---

## Could Archon Be Simplified to Match Grimwild?

### What You'd Need to Remove:

1. **Merge Boons/Banes + Complications into one "thorns" system**
   - Difficulty AND enemy defenses both add thorns (d8s)
   - Armored knight = +2 thorns
   - Wrong tool = +1 thorn
   - Total thorns on your roll

2. **Remove Edge and Expertise**
   - No post-roll modification
   - Roll → check thorns → outcome
   - Specialists just have better base pools (more dice)

3. **Freeform Consequences**
   - Instead of "1 Stress, 2 Stress, Affliction size," GM makes impact moves
   - "Mark 1 Stress" becomes "take harm" (GM describes)

4. **Keep Momentum as Spark**
   - Spend before roll to add dice
   - Gain on disasters, tangles, etc.

**Result: 5-step resolution like Grimwild**
1. Frame + Pool (stat dice + thorns)
2. Push Yourself? (spend Momentum for +1d)
3. Roll + Read Highest
4. Apply Thorns (7-8s cut)
5. GM makes Impact Move (if Messy/Grim)

---

## But What Would You Lose?

### Lost Tactical Depth

**Current Archon:**
- "This knight is Resilient + Deadly. I'll spend Momentum for Edge to cancel both, then attack."
- Tactical resource decision creating gameplay

**Simplified (like Grimwild):**
- "This knight gives 2 thorns. I'll spend Momentum for +1d to improve odds."
- Less tactical, more probabilistic

---

### Lost Mechanical Consistency

**Current Archon:**
- Complication tags are defined (Resilient applies to damage attempts)
- Consequences are structured (1 Stress = Minor, 2 Stress = Major)
- Less GM burden, more bookkeeping

**Simplified (like Grimwild):**
- GM decides thorns and impact moves on the fly
- More GM burden, less bookkeeping

---

### Lost Specialist Fantasy

**Current Archon:**
- Expertise ensures masters rarely fail in their domain
- Edge lets specialists bypass defenses others can't
- Feels different from non-specialists

**Simplified (like Grimwild):**
- Specialists just have bigger pools (3d vs 2d)
- Still fail on bad rolls (no safety net)

---

## The Core Question

**Is Archon's extra complexity (5 subsystems, 10 steps) worth:**
- ✅ Tactical depth (Edge vs. Complications)
- ✅ Mechanical consistency (less GM judgment)
- ✅ Specialist differentiation (Edge + Expertise)
- ❌ But: slower resolution, more bookkeeping

**vs. Grimwild's simplicity (3-5 steps) trading:**
- ❌ Less tactical depth (just add dice)
- ❌ More GM improvisation (invent impact moves)
- ✅ But: faster resolution, less bookkeeping

---

## My Analysis

### What Makes Grimwild Fast

1. **Single difficulty mechanism** (thorns)
2. **No post-roll layers** (just check thorns)
3. **Freeform consequences** (GM invents)
4. **Variance-based progress** (diminishing pools)

### What Makes Archon Slower

1. **Multiple difficulty mechanisms** (Boons/Banes + Complications)
2. **Post-roll modification stack** (Edge → Complications → Expertise)
3. **Structured consequences** (tiers, Afflictions)
4. **Deterministic progress** (fixed tracks)

### The Fundamental Trade-Off

**Grimwild prioritizes speed and flow.** GM judgment replaces mechanical subsystems.

**Archon prioritizes tactical depth and consistency.** Mechanical subsystems replace GM judgment.

Neither is "better"—they serve different design goals.

---

## Simplification Recommendations for Archon

If you want to move toward Grimwild's speed without losing everything:

### Option 1: Remove Expertise (Conservative)

**Impact:** One fewer resolution step (10 → 9)
**Trade-off:** Specialists fail more often (compensate with Trait granting +1 Boon)
**Complexity reduction:** ~10%

---

### Option 2: Remove Edge + Expertise (Moderate)

**Impact:** Two fewer resolution steps (10 → 8)
**Trade-off:** Less tactical depth, no "bypass defenses" gameplay
**Complexity reduction:** ~20%

Create an Opening would need to change: "Spend 1 Momentum for +2 Boons" instead of "+1 Boon + 1 Edge"

---

### Option 3: Merge Boons + Complications into Thorns (Radical)

**Impact:** Grimwild-style unified difficulty system
**Trade-off:** Lose Complication tags, lose Edge/Expertise entirely
**Complexity reduction:** ~40%

This would fundamentally change the game's identity.

---

## My Recommendation

**Keep the current system, remove only Expertise.**

**Why:**
- Complications create tactical puzzles (core differentiation from Grimwild)
- Edge rewards mastery and creates gameplay (spend Momentum to bypass defenses)
- Momentum economy is load-bearing
- Expertise is the weakest link (safety net with low payoff, late in resolution)

**Removing Expertise:**
- 10 steps → 9 steps
- Compensate by making Traits grant +1 Boon automatically
- Keeps tactical depth, loses only the "convert Setback" safety net

**This gets you 10% simpler without losing your game's core identity.**

If you want to go further (remove Edge too), you start converging toward Grimwild's model—but then ask: why not just play Grimwild?

| System | Steps | Where Complexity Lives |
|--------|-------|----------------------|
| **Grimwild** | 3-5 | GM judgment (thorns, impact moves) |
| **Archon (current)** | 10 | Mechanical subsystems (Edge, Expertise, Complications) |
| **Archon (remove Expertise)** | 9 | Same, minus one safety net |
| **Archon (remove Edge + Expertise)** | 8 | Less tactical, more like Grimwild |
| **Archon (unified thorns)** | 5 | Basically Grimwild with Archon's fiction |
