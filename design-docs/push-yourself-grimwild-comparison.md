# Rethinking Push Yourself: Grimwild Model vs. Current Archon

## How Grimwild Actually Uses Push Yourself

> "Push Yourself. Expend extra effort to activate certain talents that require it. After using the talent, and resolving any related rolls, then mark a stat (your choice). Choose your moments wisely."

**Key points:**
1. Push Yourself is NOT a baseline mechanic everyone uses
2. It's specifically a **cost for certain talents**
3. You mark harm AFTER the talent activates
4. It's a framework, not a general "spend resource for bonus" action

**Example Grimwild Talents:**
- "When you attack, you may Push Yourself to deal extra harm"
- "You may Push Yourself to teleport a short distance"

The talent defines what you get. Push Yourself is just the payment.

---

## How Archon Currently Uses Push Yourself

**Current Archon:**
- Push Yourself = Mark 1 Stress to gain +1 Boon (universal mechanic, everyone can use)
- Talents build on this: "When you Push Yourself during [X], you also [Y]"

**This is different from Grimwild:**
- Grimwild: Push is ONLY for talents (no baseline benefit)
- Archon: Push is universal (+1 Boon), talents add to it

---

## The Real Question

**Do we need a universal "mark Stress for mechanical benefit" action?**

### What Stress Spending Currently Does in Archon

1. **Push Yourself (self):** Mark 1 Stress for +1 Boon (pre-roll)
2. **Assist (ally):** Mark 1 Stress for ally's +1 Boon (pre-roll)
3. **Resist Consequence:** Spend 1 Momentum to reduce Consequence (Momentum, not Stress)

**We also have:**
- **Create an Opening:** Spend 1 Momentum for +1 Boon + 1 Edge (Momentum, not Stress)

**So for SELF-improvement, you have:**
- Push Yourself (Stress) → +1 Boon
- Create an Opening (Momentum) → +1 Boon + 1 Edge

**These overlap.** Both spend a resource for pre-roll improvement.

---

## Option 1: Remove Push Yourself Entirely (Radical)

**Keep only:**
- Assist (mark Stress for ally)
- Create an Opening (spend Momentum for self)

**Remove:**
- Push Yourself (baseline mechanic)
- Expertise (passive conversion)

**How you improve your own odds:**
1. Circumstantial Boons (GM grants for good positioning/tools)
2. Create an Opening (spend Momentum for +1 Boon + 1 Edge)
3. Invoke Trait (enables Special Effects)

**Talents that currently require "Push Yourself":**
- Rewrite as: "Mark 1 Stress to [special effect]"
- Each talent defines its own benefit
- Like Grimwild's model (Push is just the cost framework)

**Example rewrite:**
- Current: "When you Push Yourself in combat, you also gain 1 Edge"
- New: "In combat, you may mark 1 Stress to gain +1 Boon and 1 Edge"

**Pros:**
- ✅ Removes overlapping resource spend (Stress vs Momentum for same purpose)
- ✅ Makes Momentum the primary proactive resource ("spend to improve odds")
- ✅ Makes Stress purely reactive/defensive (marks from Consequences, spent for Assist)
- ✅ Simplifies: no baseline Push Yourself step
- ✅ Talents define their own costs (like Grimwild)

**Cons:**
- ❌ Major system change (high risk)
- ❌ Less Stress spending options (less tension?)
- ❌ Momentum becomes more important (are there enough sources?)

---

## Option 2: Keep Push Yourself, Remove Expertise (Conservative)

**Keep:**
- Push Yourself (mark Stress for +1 Boon, pre-roll)
- Assist (mark Stress for ally)

**Remove:**
- Expertise (passive Setback conversion)

**Don't add:**
- No "Refuse to Fail" (post-roll option)
- No specialist compensation

**Result:**
- Specialists just fail more often (no safety net)
- Simpler character building (no Expertise tracking)
- Step 9 becomes: just apply final outcome (no conversion check)

**This is the ACTUAL simplification:**
- 10 steps → effectively 9 steps (Step 9 is just "resolve outcome")
- No new mechanics added
- Specialists differentiated by: higher Approach, Traits, Talents (not Expertise)

---

## Option 3: Grimwild-Style Push (Framework Only)

**Redefine Push Yourself as Talent framework:**

**Push Yourself** = Mark 1 Stress after using a Talent that requires it (no baseline benefit)

**Remove baseline "+1 Boon for marking Stress."**

**Talents define their benefits:**
- "You may mark 1 Stress to gain +1 Boon" (some Talents do this)
- "You may mark 1 Stress to [special effect]" (other Talents do different things)
- Talents that grant "+1 Boon" effectively replace the baseline Push Yourself

**This is closer to Grimwild's model:**
- Push Yourself is just the payment mechanism
- Talents define what you're buying

**Pros:**
- ✅ More flexible (Talents can do anything)
- ✅ Closer to Grimwild's model
- ✅ No universal baseline (cleaner for people who don't have relevant Talents)

**Cons:**
- ❌ Removes universal option (only people with Talents can "Push")
- ❌ Major rewrite of Talents

## My Analysis

### The Core Issue

**You have two resources (Stress and Momentum) that can both be spent pre-roll to improve odds:**

- Stress (Push Yourself) → +1 Boon
- Momentum (Create an Opening) → +1 Boon + 1 Edge

**These overlap functionally.** Both are "spend resource to boost success chance."

**Question: Do you need both?**

### The Case for Removing Push Yourself

**Stress and Momentum should serve different purposes:**

**Momentum (proactive):**
- Spend to improve odds (Create an Opening)
- Spend to reduce Consequences (Resist)
- Offense and defense resource
- Gain from Triumphs, Build Momentum effect

**Stress (reactive/defensive):**
- Marks from Consequences (absorbs harm)
- Spend to help others (Assist)
- Purely defensive resource
- No proactive self-benefit

**This is cleaner separation:**
- Want better odds? Spend Momentum (Create an Opening)
- Want to help allies? Spend Stress (Assist)
- Stress is your buffer, Momentum is your fuel

---

### The Case for Keeping Push Yourself

**Gives players more agency:**
- Sometimes you don't have Momentum
- Sometimes allies can't/won't Assist
- Push Yourself is a last resort ("I dig deep")

**Creates Stress tension:**
- Do I spend my buffer now or save it?
- Balancing proactive spending vs. defensive reserve

**Different resource trade-offs:**
- Momentum is precious (limited sources)
- Stress regenerates (Short Rest)
- Different risk profiles

---

## My Recommendation: Option 2 (Remove Only Expertise)

**Why:**

1. **Lowest risk** - minimal system change
2. **Actual simplification** - removes Step 9 entirely (no conversion check)
3. **Preserves player options** - Push Yourself and Assist still work
4. **Preserves Talent framework** - Talents that key off Push still work
5. **Clear trade-off** - specialists fail more but game is simpler

**Don't add "Refuse to Fail."** You're right that adding a second Stress-spending option just shuffles complexity.

**Don't compensate specialists.** They're still better (higher Approach, Traits, Talents). Just not infallible.

---

## But IF You Want to Go Further...

**Option 1 (Remove Push Yourself) is worth considering if:**

- You want cleaner resource separation (Momentum = proactive, Stress = reactive)
- You're okay rewriting Talents
- You want to reduce overlapping resource spends

**This would be more radical but create cleaner design:**
- Stress = defense (absorbs harm, helps allies)
- Momentum = offense (boosts your rolls, resists consequences)
