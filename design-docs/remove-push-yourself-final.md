# Removing Push Yourself (Baseline): Final Analysis

## The Proposal

**Remove:** Push Yourself as "+1 Boon for 1 Stress" (baseline mechanic everyone uses)

**Keep:** Push Yourself as a Talent activation framework (like Grimwild)

**Some Talents cost:** "Mark 1 Stress" to activate special effects

---

## What You Currently Have for Gaining Boons

### Pre-Roll Boon Sources

1. **Circumstantial Boons** (GM grants for good position, right tools, etc.)
2. **Push Yourself** (mark 1 Stress for +1 Boon) ← Removing this
3. **Assist** (ally marks 1 Stress for your +1 Boon)
4. **Create an Opening** (spend 1 Momentum for +1 Boon + 1 Edge)
5. **Various Talents** (some grant situational Boons)

**If you remove Push Yourself (#2), you still have 4 other sources.**

---

## How Resources Would Work After Removal

### Momentum (Proactive/Offensive)

**Spend Momentum to:**
- Create an Opening (+1 Boon + 1 Edge)
- Resist Consequence (reduce severity)

**Momentum is your offensive resource** - boost rolls, reduce consequences.

**Gain Momentum from:**
- Triumphs (roll 2+ sixes)
- Build Momentum (Trait Special Effect)
- GM grants for clever plans/good roleplay

---

### Stress (Reactive/Defensive)

**Mark Stress when:**
- Consequences land (1 or 2 Stress)
- You Assist an ally (mark 1 to grant them +1 Boon)
- You activate certain Talents (mark 1 Stress as payment)

**Stress is your defensive buffer** - absorbs harm, helps allies, fuels special abilities.

**Clear Stress via:**
- Short Rest (1 hour)
- Long Rest (overnight)

---

## This Creates Clean Resource Separation

**Before (with baseline Push Yourself):**
- Stress: absorbs harm, OR spend for +1 Boon (self), OR spend for +1 Boon (ally)
- Momentum: spend for +1 Boon + Edge, OR spend to resist

Both resources can boost your odds. Overlapping function.

---

**After (without baseline Push Yourself):**
- **Stress: defensive/supportive** - absorbs harm, helps allies, fuels Talents
- **Momentum: offensive/proactive** - boosts your rolls, reduces consequences

Different functions. Cleaner design.

---

## Resolution Flow After Removal

### Current (10 Steps)

1. Frame
2. Pool
3. Boons/Banes
4. **Push Yourself?** (mark Stress for +1 Boon)
5. Roll
6. Raw Outcome
7. Edge
8. Complications
9. Expertise check
10. Final Outcome

---

### After Removing Push Yourself + Expertise (8 Steps)

1. Frame
2. Pool (Approach + Trait)
3. Boons/Banes (circumstantial)
4. ~~Push Yourself removed~~
5. Roll
6. Raw Outcome
7. Edge (cancel Complications)
8. Complications (apply remaining)
9. ~~Expertise removed~~
10. Final Outcome (mark tracks, consequences)

**Effectively 8 steps** (Steps 4 and 9 eliminated).

---

## How Talents Would Work

### Grimwild Model: Push Yourself as Cost

**Current Archon Talents that use Push Yourself:**
> "When you Push Yourself during combat, you also gain 1 Edge."

**Rewritten without baseline Push:**
> "In combat, you may mark 1 Stress to gain +1 Boon and 1 Edge."

**The Talent defines what you get. Marking Stress is just the payment.**

---

### Examples of Rewritten Talents

**Combat Talent (offensive):**
> **Surge:** When you Clash, you may mark 1 Stress to gain +1 Boon and mark 2 progress instead of 1.

**Mobility Talent:**
> **Skirmisher:** When you move in combat, you may mark 1 Stress to move 1 additional zone and ignore one enemy's ability to block your movement.

**Magical Talent:**
> **Arcane Surge:** When you cast a spell, you may mark 1 Stress to gain +1 Boon and increase the effect (as Enhanced Effect).

**Each Talent specifies:**
- When it can be used (context)
- What it costs (mark 1 Stress)
- What benefit you get

**No baseline "mark Stress = +1 Boon" needed.**

---

## What About Characters Without Relevant Talents?

**Before:** Everyone could Push Yourself for +1 Boon (universal option)

**After:** Only Talents grant Stress-spending options (not universal)

**How do you boost your odds if you don't have Talents?**

1. **Circumstantial Boons** (GM grants for good positioning, right tools)
2. **Create an Opening** (spend Momentum for +1 Boon + 1 Edge)
3. **Assist** (ally marks Stress for your +1 Boon)

**You still have 3 sources.**

**And most characters WILL have some Talents** that let them mark Stress for benefits in specific contexts.

---

## Implications for Character Power

### Early Game (Few Talents)

**Before:** Could always Push Yourself for +1 Boon

**After:** 
- Rely on circumstantial Boons (position, tools)
- Rely on Create an Opening (Momentum)
- Rely on Assist (allies)
- Maybe 1-2 Talents that let you mark Stress for specific benefits

**Slightly less powerful** (one fewer Boon source), but cleaner play.

---

### Late Game (Many Talents)

**Before:** Push Yourself for +1 Boon, plus Talent effects

**After:** Multiple Talents offering Stress-spending options in various contexts

**Example late-game character:**
- Surge (combat: mark Stress for +1 Boon + 2 progress)
- Skirmisher (mobility: mark Stress for movement + ignore blocking)
- Iron Will (resist mental effects: mark Stress for advantage)

**More interesting choices** (spend Stress on which Talent?) vs. generic "+1 Boon."

---

## What About Assist?

**Assist remains unchanged:**
> When an ally makes an Action Roll, you may mark 1 Stress to grant them +1 Boon.

**This is the one universal Stress-spending option that remains.**

**Why keep Assist but remove Push Yourself?**
- Assist encourages teamwork (design goal)
- Helps weaker characters (support role)
- Creates shared risk (you're marking Stress to help)

**Push Yourself was just "spend Stress for better odds" (overlaps with Momentum).**

---

## Impact on Stress Economy

### How Stress Currently Flows

**Marks from:**
- Consequences (1-2 per hit)
- Push Yourself (1 per use)
- Assist (1 per use)

**Clears via:**
- Short Rest (all)
- Long Rest (all)

---

### After Removing Baseline Push Yourself

**Marks from:**
- Consequences (1-2 per hit) ← primary source
- Assist (1 per use)
- Talents (1 per activation)

**Less voluntary Stress marking** (no baseline Push), so your track fills slower.

**Is this a problem?**

**Possibly not:**
- Stress should mainly mark from taking hits (Consequences)
- Voluntary marking (Assist, Talents) should be occasional
- If Stress fills too slowly, combats feel less dangerous

**Actually, this might be GOOD:**
- Current system: Players mark Stress proactively (Push Yourself), so track fills from player choices
- New system: Stress marks from getting hit (reactive), so track fills from danger

**Makes Stress feel more like a danger gauge, less like a currency.**

---

## Playstyle Changes

### Current Playstyle

**Players think:** "Should I spend Stress now (Push) or save it for later?"

**Stress is both:**
- Buffer (absorbs hits)
- Currency (spend for power)

**Tension:** spend now vs. save for defense

---

### New Playstyle

**Players think:** "Should I use Momentum to boost this roll?"

**Stress is primarily:**
- Buffer (absorbs hits)
- Occasionally spent (Assist, specific Talents)

**Momentum becomes THE proactive resource.**

**Tension:** spend Momentum now vs. save for crucial roll

---

## Momentum Becomes More Important

**If Push Yourself is removed, Momentum is the main proactive resource.**

**Do you have enough Momentum sources?**

**Current sources:**
- Triumphs (2+ sixes on a roll)
- Build Momentum (Trait Special Effect)
- GM grants for clever plans (optional)

**Triumphs are ~3-9% chance** (depending on pool size).

**Build Momentum:** Requires rolling Conflict (succeed but take consequence), then giving up progress to gain Momentum.

**If Momentum is THE resource for boosting odds, you need enough of it.**

**Possible issue:**
- Players want to Create an Opening frequently
- But only gain Momentum occasionally (Triumphs are rare)
- Creates "Momentum scarcity" problem

**Solutions:**
1. Increase Momentum capacity (currently max 3)
2. Add more Momentum sources (certain Talents grant it)
3. Make Triumphs more common (change math)
4. Accept that Create an Opening is rare/precious

**Or:**
5. Keep baseline Push Yourself so players always have a Stress-spending option

---

## The Core Trade-Off

### Pros of Removing Baseline Push Yourself

✅ **Cleaner resource design** (Stress = defense, Momentum = offense)

✅ **Fewer decision points** (no "should I Push?" every roll)

✅ **8 steps instead of 10** (removing Push + Expertise)

✅ **More interesting Talents** (Stress-spending is contextual, not universal)

✅ **Stress feels more like danger** (marks from getting hit, not player spending)

---

### Cons of Removing Baseline Push Yourself

❌ **Less player agency** (fewer ways to proactively boost odds)

❌ **Momentum scarcity** (it's now THE proactive resource, but sources are limited)

❌ **Characters without Talents feel weaker** (no universal Stress-spending option)

❌ **Might need to adjust Momentum economy** (add sources or increase capacity)

---

## My Analysis

**This is a good change IF:**
1. You're okay with Momentum being the primary proactive resource
2. You add more Momentum sources (or increase capacity)
3. You want cleaner resource separation (Stress = defense, Momentum = offense)

**This is risky IF:**
1. Momentum sources are too limited (creates scarcity problem)
2. Players expect to "spend resources for power" every turn
3. You haven't playtested the Momentum economy thoroughly

---

## Recommendation: Conditional Yes

**I think this is a good change, with one condition:**

**You need to ensure Momentum economy can support it.**

**Options:**

**A. Increase Momentum capacity** (max 3 → max 4 or 5)
- More headroom to save up
- Create an Opening is less "do I spend my only Momentum?"

**B. Add Momentum sources**
- Certain Talents grant Momentum (like Build Momentum does)
- GM grants Momentum for clever solutions/good roleplay
- Maybe: Start each session with 1 Momentum

**C. Accept that Create an Opening is rare**
- It's a precious resource (like Hero Points in other games)
- Players use it for crucial rolls only
- Compensation: Stress-based Talents give more frequent power spikes

---

## Final Resolution Flow (8 Steps)

### After Removing Push Yourself (Baseline) + Expertise

1. **Frame** (GM sets Vantage, Stakes)
2. **Determine Pool** (Approach + Trait if invoked)
3. **Apply Boons/Banes** (circumstantial, ±2 cap)
4. ~~Push Yourself removed~~
5. **Roll Dice** (all dice in pool)
6. **Determine Raw Outcome** (count results)
7. **Spend Edge** (cancel Complications if available)
8. **Apply Complications** (reduce outcome by remaining)
9. ~~Expertise removed~~
10. **Apply Final Outcome** (Consequences, mark tracks)

**Effective step count: 8**

**Decision points:**
- Before roll: Should I Create an Opening? (Momentum)
- After roll: None (just apply outcome)

**Cleaner than current 10 steps.**

### Remove

> ~~When you make an Action Roll, you may mark 1 Stress to gain +1 Boon.~~

**Expertise:**
> ~~When your final outcome is Setback while acting in your domain of Expertise, convert it to Conflict.~~

### Keep / Add

**Push Yourself (framework):**
> Some Talents require you to mark Stress to activate them. This represents pushing beyond your limits for special effects.

**Assist (unchanged):**
> When an ally makes an Action Roll, you may mark 1 Stress to grant them +1 Boon.

**Create an Opening (unchanged):**
> Spend 1 Momentum to gain +1 Boon and +1 Edge before your next Action Roll.

---

### Update Talents

**Rewrite Talents to define their own Stress-spending benefits:**

**Before:**
> "When you Push Yourself during combat, you also gain 1 Edge."

**After:**
> "In combat, you may mark 1 Stress to gain +1 Boon and 1 Edge."

**Each Talent now specifies what you get for marking Stress.**

---

## Conclusion

**Remove baseline Push Yourself (+1 Boon for Stress).**

**Keep it as a framework for Talents.**

**Result:**
- ✅ 10 → 8 steps (remove Step 4 and Step 9)
- ✅ Cleaner resource design (Momentum = proactive, Stress = defensive)
- ✅ More interesting Talents (contextual Stress-spending)
- ⚠️ Requires adequate Momentum economy (add sources or increase capacity)

**This is a strong simplification move that also improves design clarity.**

**Combine with removing Expertise** for maximum impact (8 steps total).
