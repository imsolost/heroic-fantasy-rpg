# Archon Resolution: Step-by-Step Necessity Analysis

## Current 10-Step Process

Let's examine each step and justify whether it's truly necessary.

---

## Step 1: Frame Action (GM sets Stakes, Vantage)

**What happens:** Player describes intent and approach. GM assesses Vantage and sets Stakes.

**Is it necessary?** YES - Load-bearing

**Why:**
- Vantage determines what's possible (Certain/Strong/Standard/Limited/Impossible)
- Stakes determine if the action is risky enough to roll
- This is fundamental to any RPG resolution

**Grimwild equivalent:** Same - assess Vantage, determine if risky

**Can it be simplified?** No. This is irreducible.

---

## Step 2: Determine Pool (Approach + Trait if invoked)

**What happens:** Count base dice (Approach rating) + add dice from invoked Trait

**Is it necessary?** YES - Load-bearing

**Why:**
- You need to know how many dice to roll
- Approach = your core competency (1-3d6)
- Trait invocation = "I'm leaning on my defining characteristic"

**Grimwild equivalent:** Roll stat dice (1-3d6)

**Can it be simplified?** 
- Maybe: Could Trait invocation be baked into Approach rather than optional?
- Current: "I have Daring 2. If I invoke my Trait, I roll 3d6."
- Alternative: "I have Daring 2, which includes my Trait. I always roll 3d6 when using Daring."

**Problem with simplification:** Loses the "leaning on your identity" moment. Traits become passive rather than active choices.

**Verdict:** Keep as-is. Trait invocation is a meaningful player choice.

---

## Step 3: Apply Boons/Banes (circumstantial, capped at ±2)

**What happens:** GM adds/subtracts dice based on circumstances (good position, right tools, allies helping, etc.)

**Is it necessary?** YES - Load-bearing

**Why:**
- GM needs a difficulty dial
- Circumstances matter (fighting downhill vs. uphill, right tool vs. wrong tool)
- The ±2 cap prevents runaway bonuses

**Grimwild equivalent:** Thorns (add d8s for difficulty)

**Difference:** Grimwild uses additive thorns (add bad dice), Archon uses pool modification (add/remove good dice)

**Can it be simplified?** 
- Could merge with Complications? (see Step 8)
- Current: Boons/Banes = circumstances, Complications = enemy defenses
- Merged: Everything adds thorns (d8s) like Grimwild

**Problem with merging:** Loses granularity. "You have the right tool" (circumstantial advantage) feels different from "the enemy has tough armor" (defensive ability).

**Verdict:** Keep as-is. Circumstantial difficulty is fundamental to RPGs.

---

## Step 4: Push Yourself? (mark 1 Stress for +1 Boon)

**What happens:** Player decides whether to spend 1 Stress for +1 Boon

**Is it necessary?** DEBATABLE

**Why it exists:**
- Player agency - "I dig deep"
- Resource economy - spend resilience for power
- Creates tension - do I spend Stress now or save it?

**Grimwild equivalent:** Spark (add +1d before roll) OR Push (mark harm to remove 1d from pool after roll if no dice removed)

**Wait, Grimwild's "Push" is different:**
- Grimwild Push: Happens AFTER rolling a task pool with no dice removed. Mark harm to force 1d removal.
- Archon Push Yourself: Happens BEFORE rolling. Mark Stress for +1 Boon.

**Can it be simplified?**
- Could remove it entirely - Stress only marks from Consequences
- Would lose: Player decision point, resource tension
- Would gain: One fewer step

**Alternative:** Make it implicit in Trait invocation?
- Current: Invoke Trait → unlock Approach. Optionally Push Yourself → +1 Boon.
- New: Invoke Trait → unlock Approach + auto +1 Boon + mark 1 Stress
- This makes Trait invocation always costly but always powerful

**Problem:** Forces stress marking even when you don't want it. Current system lets you invoke Trait without spending Stress.

**Verdict:** KEEP, but it's on the bubble. It's a meaningful decision point, but adds a step.

---

## Step 5: Roll Dice

**What happens:** Roll all dice in pool, count results

**Is it necessary?** YES - Obviously load-bearing

**Why:** You need to roll to determine outcome

**Grimwild equivalent:** Same

**Can it be simplified?** No. This is irreducible.

---

## Step 6: Determine Raw Outcome (Triumph/Success/Conflict/Setback)

**What happens:** Count 6s, 4-5s, 1-3s to determine raw outcome before modifiers

**Is it necessary?** YES - Load-bearing

**Why:** You need a baseline result before applying modifiers

**Grimwild equivalent:** Read highest die (6/4-5/1-3)

**Can it be simplified?** 
- Archon uses "count 6s for Triumph, at least one 6 for Success, 4-5 for Conflict, all low for Setback"
- Could simplify to "highest die" like Grimwild?

**Problem:** Triumph requires counting (2+ sixes), which is a distinctive feature. You can't determine Triumph from "highest die."

**Verdict:** Keep as-is. Four-tier outcomes create meaningful gradation.

---

## Step 7: Spend Edge (cancel Complications before they apply)

**What happens:** If you have Edge (from Create an Opening, Talents), declare which Complications to cancel

**Is it necessary?** DEBATABLE

**Why it exists:**
- Tactical depth - "I bypass their armor"
- Reward for positioning/setup
- Interacts with Momentum economy
- Creates specialist gameplay (some Talents grant Edge)

**Grimwild equivalent:** None. Thorns just apply, you can't cancel them.

**Wait, what about Grimwild's Critical bonuses?**
- Grimwild Critical: Ignores all thorns automatically on multiple 6s
- Archon Triumph: Ignores all Complications automatically
- These are equivalent!

**But Archon also has Edge (manual Complication cancellation):**
- Spend 1 Momentum via Create an Opening → get 1 Edge
- Spend Edge to cancel Complications
- This doesn't exist in Grimwild

**Can it be simplified?**
- Remove Edge entirely
- Create an Opening would just grant "+2 Boons" instead of "+1 Boon + 1 Edge"
- Complications always apply (unless Triumph)

**What would you lose:**
- Tactical "bypass defenses" gameplay
- Specialist differentiation (Talents that grant Edge)
- Reason to spend Momentum against heavily-defended enemies

**What would you gain:**
- One fewer step
- Simpler Complication resolution (they just apply)

**Verdict:** KEEP, but this is negotiable. Edge creates tactical gameplay that differentiates Archon from Grimwild.

---

## Step 8: Apply Remaining Complications (each reduces outcome by 1 tier)

**What happens:** Check which enemy Complication tags apply to this action. Each uncancelled Complication reduces outcome by 1 tier.

**Is it necessary?** DEBATABLE

**Why it exists:**
- Enemy differentiation (Resilient foe vs. Elusive foe)
- Tactical puzzles (which approach faces fewer Complications?)
- Mechanical clarity (tags define when they apply)

**Grimwild equivalent:** Thorns apply to the roll based on situation

**Key difference:**
- Grimwild thorns are added pre-roll (they're in your pool)
- Archon Complications apply post-roll (they reduce your outcome)

**This is a HUGE difference:**
- Grimwild: 3d6 + 2 thorns → roll → check if thorns cut
- Archon: 3d6 → roll → Success → Complications reduce to Conflict

**Why does Archon use post-roll reduction?**
- Allows Edge to cancel them (tactical choice)
- Allows Expertise to convert afterward (safety net)
- Creates the "modification stack" (Step 7-8-9)

**Can it be simplified?**

**Option A: Make Complications pre-roll (like Grimwild thorns)**
- Enemy has Resilient + Deadly → add 2 thorns (d8s) to your roll
- Roll your pool + thorns, check if thorns cut
- No Edge cancellation, no post-roll modification

**What changes:**
- Removes Edge mechanic (no more tactical cancellation)
- Removes Step 7 entirely
- Simplifies to: Roll → Check thorns → Done

**Option B: Remove Complications entirely, use Banes instead**
- Current: Boons/Banes = circumstances, Complications = enemy defenses
- New: Everything is Boons/Banes
- "Fighting a heavily armored foe" = -1 Bane (GM judgment)

**What changes:**
- No mechanical tags (Resilient, Deadly, etc.)
- More GM judgment, less bookkeeping
- Basically becomes Grimwild's model (GM adds thorns for difficulty)

**Option C: Keep Complications as-is**
- They create tactical puzzles
- They differentiate enemies mechanically
- They justify Edge mechanic

**Verdict:** KEEP if you keep Edge. REMOVE if you remove Edge. They're linked.

---

## Step 9: Apply Expertise (convert Setback → Conflict if in your domain)

**What happens:** If your final outcome is Setback and you have Expertise in this domain, convert to Conflict

**Is it necessary?** NO - Weakest link

**Why it exists:**
- Safety net for specialists
- "Masters rarely fail"
- Raises outcome floor

**Grimwild equivalent:** None. You just fail on Grim.

**Can it be simplified?**
- Remove entirely
- Compensate by making Traits grant +1 Boon (raises floor via dice pool instead)

**What would you lose:**
- Setback → Conflict conversion (safety net)
- One resolution step

**What would you gain:**
- Simpler resolution (10 → 9 steps)
- Cleaner character building (no separate Expertise tracking)

**Alternative (as discussed earlier):**
- Change Expertise to grant +1 Edge instead of converting Setback
- Happens at Step 7 (with Edge), not Step 9
- Same math, earlier timing

**Verdict:** REMOVE or REDEFINE. This is the most cuttable step.

---

## Step 10: Apply Final Outcome (Consequence + mark tracks)

**What happens:** Apply the final outcome (mark tracks, apply Consequences)

**Is it necessary?** YES - Load-bearing

**Why:** You need to resolve the action's effects

**Grimwild equivalent:** Same - apply outcome, GM makes impact move if prompted

**Can it be simplified?** No. This is irreducible.

---

## Summary: Which Steps Are Negotiable?

| Step | Necessary? | Reason |
|------|-----------|---------|
| 1. Frame | YES | Load-bearing (Vantage, Stakes) |
| 2. Determine Pool | YES | Load-bearing (need dice count) |
| 3. Boons/Banes | YES | Load-bearing (difficulty dial) |
| 4. Push Yourself | DEBATABLE | Creates decision point, but adds step |
| 5. Roll | YES | Irreducible |
| 6. Raw Outcome | YES | Load-bearing (baseline result) |
| 7. Spend Edge | DEBATABLE | Tactical depth, linked to Complications |
| 8. Apply Complications | DEBATABLE | Enemy differentiation, linked to Edge |
| 9. Apply Expertise | NO | Weakest link, safety net with late timing |
| 10. Final Outcome | YES | Irreducible |

**Load-bearing (can't remove): Steps 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10 = 6 steps**

**Negotiable: Steps 4, 7, 8, 9 = 4 steps**

---

## Simplification Paths

### Path 1: Remove Only Expertise (Conservative)

**Remove:** Step 9
**Keep:** Everything else

**Result:** 10 → 9 steps

**Trade-off:**
- ✅ Simpler
- ✅ Keeps tactical depth (Complications + Edge)
- ❌ Specialists fail more often (compensate with Trait granting +1 Boon)

---

### Path 2: Remove Edge + Expertise (Moderate)

**Remove:** Step 7 (Edge), Step 9 (Expertise)
**Modify:** Step 8 (Complications just apply, no cancellation)
**Keep:** Complications still exist as tags

**Result:** 10 → 8 steps

**Trade-off:**
- ✅ Simpler
- ✅ Complications still differentiate enemies
- ❌ No tactical "bypass defenses" gameplay
- ❌ Create an Opening needs new effect (just "+2 Boons"?)

---

### Path 3: Merge Complications into Banes (Aggressive)

**Remove:** Step 7 (Edge), Step 8 (Complications), Step 9 (Expertise)
**Modify:** Step 3 (Banes now include enemy defenses, GM judgment)
**Result:** 10 → 7 steps

**Trade-off:**
- ✅ Much simpler
- ✅ Closer to Grimwild model
- ❌ No mechanical enemy tags (more GM judgment)
- ❌ No tactical depth
- ❌ Loses core identity

---

### Path 4: Make Push Yourself Implicit (Alternative)

**Remove:** Step 4 as separate decision
**Modify:** Trait invocation auto-grants +1 Boon (costs 1 Stress)

**Result:** 10 → 9 steps (but different than removing Expertise)

**Trade-off:**
- ✅ One fewer decision point
- ❌ Forces Stress spend on Trait invocation
- ❌ Loses "Push Yourself" as standalone option

---

## My Recommendation: Two-Stage Simplification

### Stage 1 (Low Risk): Remove Expertise

**Change:** Remove Step 9 (Expertise)
**Compensation:** Make Traits grant +1 Boon when invoked
**Result:** 9 steps
**Complexity reduction:** ~11%

This is the safest cut. Expertise is the weakest link:
- Happens late (step 9)
- Overlaps with Boons (both raise floor)
- Safety net is less exciting than tactical tools

---

### Stage 2 (Medium Risk): Evaluate Edge + Complications

**After removing Expertise, playtest and ask:**
- Is the Edge vs. Complications tactical layer worth the complexity?
- Do players engage with "bypass defenses" gameplay?
- Are Complication tags useful for GMs?

**If YES:** Keep Steps 7-8 as-is.
**If NO:** Consider Path 2 (remove Edge, keep Complications) or Path 3 (remove both).

---

## The Core Question

**What is Archon's identity?**

**Option A: Tactical depth RPG**
- Complications create puzzles
- Edge creates solutions
- Momentum spending matters
- Keep Steps 7-8

**Option B: Streamlined heroic fantasy**
- Simpler resolution
- More GM judgment, less bookkeeping
- Remove Steps 7-8-9

**You can't be both.** Pick one, then commit.

## What Would Grimwild-Level Simplification Look Like?

**Grimwild's actual steps:**
1. Frame (Vantage assessment)
2. Determine pool (stat dice + thorns for difficulty)
3. Assist? (allies add 1d, share risk)
4. Spark? (spend spark for +1d)
5. Roll + read highest
6. Check thorns (7-8 cuts)
7. Apply outcome / GM makes impact move

**That's 7 steps if you use everything.**

**Archon to match this:**
1. Frame (Vantage)
2. Determine pool (Approach + Trait)
3. Push Yourself? (mark Stress for +1d)
4. Banes? (add thorns [d8s] for difficulty, including enemy defenses)
5. Roll + read highest
6. Check thorns (7-8 cuts)
7. Apply outcome + Consequences

**This requires:**
- Merge Boons/Banes + Complications into thorns (Step 3 → Step 4)
- Remove Edge (Step 7)
- Remove Expertise (Step 9)
- Use "highest die" instead of "count outcomes" (Step 6 simplified)

**You'd be 90% Grimwild at that point.**

---

## Recommendation

**Remove Expertise (Step 9) and see how it feels.**

Don't touch Steps 7-8 (Edge + Complications) yet. They're your core differentiation. If you remove them, you're just Grimwild with different fiction.

Playtest with 9 steps. If it still feels complex, THEN consider whether Edge/Complications are worth it.

But start with the easy win: kill Expertise.
