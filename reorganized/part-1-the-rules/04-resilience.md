# Chapter 4: Resilience

Heroes take hits — physical blows, emotional strain, magical backlash, exhaustion. Chapter 3 taught you how Tracks work. This chapter covers how the three **Character Tracks** work in play.

**What you'll learn:**
- Stress (your resilience buffer)
- Afflictions (temporary impairments)
- Burdens (lasting harm)
- Taken Out (what happens when you break)
- Push Yourself (activating Talents)
- Recovery (Short Rest and Long Rest)

---

**Quick reference from Chapter 3:**

| Track | Size | Marks When... | Full Means... | Clears When... |
|-------|------|---------------|---------------|----------------|
| **Stress** | 5 boxes | Consequence (1/2) or Push Yourself (Talents) | Take Burden or be Taken Out | Short Rest or Long Rest |
| **Affliction** | 1-3 boxes | Relevant Action Roll (any outcome) | Cleared — worked through it | Short Rest (even if not full) |
| **Burden** | 1-3 boxes | Long Rest or narrative condition | Cleared — recovered | When track fills |

This chapter explains:
- **Stress:** Push Yourself (Talents), Overflow math, Taken Out
- **Afflictions:** Relevance judgment, common names, stacking rules
- **Burdens:** Recovery types, gaining sources, narrative restrictions
- **Recovery:** Short Rest vs Long Rest timing

---

## Stress

**Stress is your primary resilience buffer** — it absorbs incoming Consequences and represents getting tired, rattled, taking a glancing wound, or pushing beyond normal limits.

**Stress is unique among Tracks:** It's the only one you can mark voluntarily for immediate mechanical benefits.

### Stress Track Basics

- **5 boxes** (all characters)
- Mark boxes left to right
- Marks from two sources:
  - **Consequences:** 1 box (Minor), 2 boxes (Major)
  - **Push Yourself (Talents):** 1 box (when activating a Talent)

**When full and you'd mark more:** See "Stress Overflow" below.

---

### Push Yourself

**Push Yourself** to activate certain Talents that require it. After using the Talent and resolving any related rolls, mark 1 Stress (unless the Talent waives this cost). Choose your moments wisely.

**You can only Push Yourself once per Action Roll.** If you have multiple Talents that could trigger when you Push Yourself in the current situation, choose one to activate.

When you Push Yourself, describe how you're digging deep—forcing past exhaustion, tapping hidden reserves, or committing everything to this moment.

**Without a Talent that uses Push Yourself, you cannot Push Yourself.** Push Yourself has no baseline effect—it exists only to activate specific Talents.

**Example Talents:**
> **Surge:** When you Clash, you may Push Yourself to gain +1 Boon and mark 2 progress instead of 1. (Mark 1 Stress after resolving the roll.)

> **Battleborn [ ] [ ]:** You have 1 Edge in combat. Mark this checkbox to Push Yourself without marking Stress.

**See Chapter 9 for complete Talent lists.**

---

### Stress Overflow

When your Stress track is full (5/5) and you would mark more Stress:

**Step 1:** Calculate overflow (how much Stress couldn't fit)

**Step 2:** Choose: **Take a Burden** or **be Taken Out** (see below)

**Step 3:** If you take a Burden:
- Overflow amount determines Burden size (1 overflow = 1-box Burden, 2 = 2-box, 3+ = 3-box)
- Name the Burden fictionally (usually "Wounded" unless context suggests otherwise)
- Clear **3 Stress** (always flat) — you have breathing room again

**Example:**
> Finn has 4/5 Stress. An ogre Clashes him — Setback means Major Consequence: mark 2 Stress.
>
> Finn marks 1 Stress (fills box 5) → **5/5 Stress**.  
> 1 Stress remains unmarked → **1 overflow**.  
> Finn chooses to take a Burden → **Wounded 1 (Burden)**.  
> Finn clears 3 Stress → **2/5 Stress**.

**What if Finn had 5/5 Stress instead?**  
The full 2 Stress is overflow (can't mark any) → Wounded 2 (Burden), then clear 3 Stress → 2/5 Stress.

**What if overflow is 3 or more?**  
Wounded 3 (Burden) — the maximum Burden size.

## Afflictions (Temporary Impairments)

**Afflictions** are temporary impairments — poison, fear, blindness, exhaustion. They're short-term states heroes shake off through action and rest.

An Affliction is a **Character Track** (see Ch. 3) with a **name** (Poisoned, Frightened, Blinded) and a **size** (1–3 boxes), both set at infliction.

### How Afflictions Work

**Each Affliction applies 1 Bane to relevant Action Rolls.** When an Affliction applies, mark 1 box on its track. When the track fills, the Affliction clears — you've fought through it.

**Think of it as "progress toward recovery."** Each time you act through the Affliction, you're closer to shaking it off.

| Size | How It Works |
|------|--------------|
| **1-box** | Apply 1 Bane on first relevant roll, mark 1 box → Affliction clears |
| **2-box** | Apply 1 Bane on first relevant roll, mark 1 box. Apply 1 Bane on second relevant roll, mark 1 box → Affliction clears |
| **3-box** | Apply 1 Bane on first three relevant rolls (mark 1 box each time) → Affliction clears after the third |

**Afflictions always clear on Short Rest**, regardless of how many boxes remain unmarked.

---

### What Does "Relevant" Mean?

An Affliction only applies (and only ticks down) when it would **fictionally affect the action**.

**Clear cases where it applies:**
- **Poisoned** affects combat, physical exertion, Clashing, Defying physical obstacles
- **Frightened** affects rolls involving the source of fear, fleeing, standing ground
- **Blinded** affects sight-dependent actions — combat, navigation, reading, Sensing visually
- **Wounded** affects strenuous physical actions — fighting, climbing, running

**Clear cases where it doesn't apply:**
- **Poisoned** doesn't affect recalling lore (Know) or calm conversation (Talk)
- **Frightened** (of a dragon) doesn't affect persuading a merchant in town
- **Blinded** doesn't affect recalling facts or hearing-based Sense rolls

**The borderline case:**  
You're **Frightened 2** (dragon's roar) and you're trying to Talk a panicked crowd into orderly evacuation. Does your fear undermine your voice, or is it unrelated?

**When in doubt, apply it.** The player takes 1 Bane now, and the Affliction ticks down one action sooner. "When in doubt, apply" isn't a punishment — it's how Afflictions clear.

---

### Multiple Afflictions

**Multiple Afflictions stack** — each applying its own 1 Bane. You can have Poisoned 2, Frightened 1, and Blinded 1 simultaneously — that's 3 Banes total.

The **±2 pool modifier cap** still applies, so 3 Afflictions = 3 Banes worth of penalty, but only **-2d** to your pool.

---

### Refresh, Not Stack

**The same Affliction cannot stack beyond its initial size.** If you're Poisoned 2 and would become Poisoned again, it **refreshes to Poisoned 2** — it doesn't become Poisoned 3 or 4.

The fictional justification: you're already fighting off poison; another dose refreshes the severity but doesn't compound it beyond what you're already enduring.

---

### Common Affliction Names

**Physical:** Poisoned, Burned, Blinded, Deafened, Restrained, Stunned, Bleeding, Weakened, Slowed, Exhausted

**Mental/Emotional:** Frightened, Enraged, Confused, Charmed, Taunted, Demoralized, Distracted

**Positional/Tactical:** Prone, Disarmed, Grappled, Pinned, Exposed, Off-Balance

---

### Gaining Afflictions

Afflictions come from two sources:

**1. Consequences:** The GM may inflict an Affliction (size 1 for Minor, size 2-3 for Major/Severe) instead of or alongside marking Stress.

**2. Challenge Moves:** Enemy abilities inflict Afflictions directly, at a size determined by the move (see Chapter 13).

---

## Burdens (Lasting Harm)

**Burdens** are persistent impairments — serious wounds, lasting trauma, powerful curses. They represent harm too significant to shake off with rest alone.

A Burden is a **Character Track** (see Ch. 3) with a **name** (Wounded, Cursed, Haunted), a **size** (1–3 boxes), and a **recovery method** (Long Rest or narrative condition).

### How Burdens Work

**Each Burden applies 1 Bane to relevant actions** (same relevance judgment as Afflictions — subject to ±2 cap). Multiple Burdens stack.

**Unlike Afflictions, Burdens don't tick down through action.** They persist until their specific recovery condition is met.

---

### The Two Recovery Types

**Long Rest Recovery (default):**  
Mark 1 box per Long Rest. When the track fills, the Burden clears.

- **Wounded 3:** Takes 3 Long Rests to clear (mark 1 box each night)
- **Wounded 1:** Takes 1 Long Rest to clear
- **Cursed 2:** If it uses Long Rest recovery, takes 2 nights

**Narrative Recovery:**  
The Burden persists until a specific fictional condition is met — rest alone won't help. When the condition is met, mark boxes accordingly (GM determines how many based on the action taken).

Examples:
- **Cursed 2** — Requires finding a priest or breaking the ritual. Mark 1 box per curse-breaking attempt that succeeds. When both boxes are filled, curse clears.
- **Poisoned 3 (Wyvern Venom)** — Requires antivenom. Rest does nothing. When antivenom is administered, mark all 3 boxes immediately → Burden clears.
- **Haunted 2** — Requires confronting the source of trauma or spiritual cleansing. Each successful confrontation marks 1 box.

**The GM always tells you which recovery type applies** when you gain a Burden:
> "That's Wounded 2 — it'll reduce 1 tier per Long Rest."  
> "That's Cursed 2 — rest won't help, you'll need to break the curse."

---

### Gaining Burdens

**1. Stress Overflow (most common):**  
When your Stress track is full and you'd mark more, choose to take a Burden instead of being Taken Out. Overflow amount determines size (see "Stress Overflow" above).

**2. Severe Consequences (Disaster):**  
The GM may assign a Burden alongside other catastrophic effects.

**3. Death Moves:**  
When significant enemies are defeated, they may trigger a final action that leaves a lasting mark. Death Move Burdens are size 2 by default.

**4. Narrative Events:**  
Divine punishment, prolonged dark magic exposure, major psychological trauma.

---

### Narrative Restrictions

**Burdens may carry fictional restrictions beyond the mechanical Bane.**

- **Wounded** limits strenuous activity — you can't sprint for hours or lift heavy loads
- **Cursed** might cause NPCs to react with fear or suspicion
- **Haunted** might trigger narrative complications when you're alone or in the dark

These are separate from the 1 Bane penalty and should be adjudicated fictionally by the GM.

---

## Taken Out

You are **Taken Out** when:
- Your Stress track is full and you choose not to (or cannot) take a Burden
- The GM rules it from overwhelming sources (Disaster, Death Moves, narrative)

**The GM narrates what happens** based on context — unconscious, fleeing in terror, captured, collapsing from exhaustion. **Taken Out is not automatically death.**

### Re-entering the Scene

A Taken Out character may re-enter when dramatically appropriate:
- Rescued by allies
- The immediate threat passes
- The fiction allows recovery (you wake up, shake off the fear, escape your bonds)

**When you recover, your Stress clears fully.** You're back in the action with 0/5 Stress, though you may have gained a Burden from the experience.

---

## Recovery

### Short Rest (1 hour, safe location)

**Clears:**
- All Stress (back to 0/5)
- All Afflictions (regardless of remaining boxes)

**Does NOT clear:**
- Burdens (persist through Short Rest)

---

### Long Rest (overnight, safe location)

**Clears:**
- All Stress (back to 0/5)
- All Afflictions (regardless of remaining boxes)

**Advances Burdens:**
- Mark 1 box on each Burden with Long Rest recovery
- When a Burden's track fills, it clears

---

### Pacing Recovery

**Short Rests** should happen:
- After significant combat
- During travel breaks or brief respites
- When players seek safety and have ~1 hour

Afflictions remain relevant within a scene but don't carry over unnecessarily between scenes.

**Long Rests** should happen:
- After returning to safety (town, camp, stronghold)
- During extended downtime
- When camping in a secure location overnight

## Example in Play

> **GM:** The assassin's poisoned blade nicks you. Poisoned 2. Mark the track.
>
> **Player:** I Clash — drive them back.
>
> **GM:** The poison affects your movement. 1 Bane. Roll it.
>
> **Player:** Conflict. (Marks 1 box on Poisoned 2 track → 1/2 boxes filled)
>
> **GM:** You slam them against the wall. They strike back — Poisoned Blade triggers. You're already Poisoned 2, so it refreshes to Poisoned 2. Mark 1 Stress.
>
> **Player:** I attack again.
>
> **GM:** Still 1 Bane from Poisoned 2. Roll it.
>
> **Player:** Success. (Marks 1 box → 2/2 boxes filled → Poisoned clears)
>
> **GM:** Solid hit. You shake off the poison and the assassin crumples.
>
> *(After combat, the party takes a Short Rest. Stress clears.)*

### Stress Overflow Example

> Finn has **4/5 Stress** marked. An ogre Clashes him — Setback means Major Consequence: mark 2 Stress.
>
> **Step 1:** Finn marks 1 Stress (fills box 5). He now has 5/5 Stress.
>
> **Step 2:** 1 Stress remains unmarked. That's **1 overflow**.
>
> **Step 3:** Finn chooses: take a **Burden** or be **Taken Out**.
>
> **Step 4:** Finn takes the Burden. Overflow is 1, so: **Wounded 1 (Burden)**.
>
> **Step 5:** Finn clears 3 Stress (always flat). His track goes from 5/5 → **2/5 Stress**.
>
> Finn is hurt but has breathing room. The Burden persists until Long Rest (then it clears).

## Design Notes: Why Resilience Works This Way

**Unified Stress simplifies tracking.** One buffer for all harm types means less bookkeeping and faster play. Physical, mental, social — it all taxes the same resource.

**Afflictions create tactical texture.** They impose Banes but tick down through engagement — you can't just wait them out mid-scene. Fighting through an Affliction costs you but also clears it.

**Burdens are rare and weighty.** They signal that something has genuinely changed for the character. The choice between Burden and Taken Out creates dramatic moments.

**Push Yourself activates special abilities.** Stress can be spent to activate Talents, granting powerful contextual effects. This makes Stress both a defensive buffer and fuel for exceptional actions.
